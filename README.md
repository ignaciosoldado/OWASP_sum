# About OWASP

The OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project) is an open, volunteer-driven community focused on helping organizations build, buy, and maintain software that can actually be trusted. It is not a vendor and does not sell products; that independence from commercial pressure is core to its identity and lets it publish security guidance without a sales motive attached.

## What the project produces

Almost everything OWASP releases is free and open to anyone, regardless of membership or affiliation. That includes application security tools, standards documents, secure-coding and testing references, training materials, cheat sheets covering dozens of specific technical topics, and recorded talks from its events. Local chapters meet around the world (both in person and online), and the foundation runs conferences and training sessions throughout the year. Nearly everyone involved (board members, chapter leaders, project maintainers) contributes as a volunteer.

## Philosophy

OWASP frames application security as a combination of people, process, and technology, on the reasoning that fixing only one of the three rarely moves the needle on its own. A team can have excellent tools and still ship insecure software if the process around code review and requirements-gathering is broken, just as a great process can't compensate for a team that's never been trained on what to look for.

## Governance and licensing

The OWASP Foundation is the nonprofit entity responsible for the project's long-term continuity. The Top 10:2025 document itself is released under a Creative Commons Attribution Share-Alike 4.0 license, meaning it can be reused and redistributed freely as long as the license terms are made clear to whoever receives it.
